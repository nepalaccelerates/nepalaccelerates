You were born at the roof of the world, and somehow they convinced you that you lived in its basement.

Consider what it takes to diminish a people who were never colonised, who absorbed every earthquake, who fixed the ropes to the top of the world so that other people could plant their flags on it. Consider what it takes to make those people feel small.

112,593 student NOCs in FY 2023/24. 14 governments since 2008, none finished a term.

It took generations of being taught that the real world was somewhere else. It took watching the brightest leave, 112,593 of them on student visas in a single year, and calling it a success. It took decades of aid that taught dependence, fourteen governments since 2008 with none finishing a term, and development plans drafted in languages our grandmothers never learned to read.

It took all of that, year after year, without rest.

And still,
they couldn't break us.

You're still here. Still fighting. Still reading this at some ungodly hour because something in you refuses to go quiet.

That refusal is not a small thing. It is everything.

Here is what I believe.

USD 57 million of foreign investment in 2024. About one billion of software exports in 2025.

I believe Nepal is not behind. Nepal is early.

I believe the very conditions that crushed us, the broken roads, the rot in the institutions, the being locked out of every system that called itself universal, forged us into something the comfortable world never had to become. Resourceful beyond what is reasonable. Patient beyond what is logical. Creative in ways no framework can hold, because the frameworks were written for people who started with more.

We are not behind. We are the first draft of what comes next.

247,882 businesses owned by women. 29.8 percent, 2018.

I believe in the woman in Butwal who runs a business that one generation ago would have needed a bank, a building and a lawyer, and who needs none of them now.

I believe in the engineer in Kathmandu solving, at midnight, a problem the rest of the world will find in five years and call new.

I believe in the farmer in Mustang who carries more knowledge of his soil in his hands than any consultant will ever fit in a report.

I believe in the student who could never afford the right school and taught herself everything on a cracked screen and borrowed wifi, and who is quietly, furiously becoming someone nobody predicted.

I believe in every Nepali who was told this country would hold them back and decided instead to drag it forward.

I believe in you. Not the version that left. Not the version still planning to. Not some polished future self. This one. Scarred, exhausted, still burning.

Here is the truth no one in this country says out loud.

We are capable of things this place has never witnessed.

Not eventually. Not once the roads are paved and the politics settle and the stars line up into something that looks like permission.

Load shedding ended in 2018. Winter power is still bought from India.

Now. With roads that wash out every monsoon, with winter power still bought from India, with wifi that dies in the middle of everything that matters.

PayPal and Stripe: still not here. 2026.

The bottleneck was never talent. This country sold close to a billion dollars of software to the world last year without PayPal, without Stripe, from bedrooms and rented rooms.

The bottleneck is belief. We carry an old, deep, collective wound that whispers: not us, not here, not yet.

This manifesto is a blade pressed against that wound.

n/acc is not a brand. It is a line drawn in time. Before, and after.

Before, we exported our people and called it opportunity. After, we build here and call it the beginning.

Before, we waited for systems that were never designed to include us. After, we build the systems and decide who gets a seat.

Before, Nepal was a place people left to become something. After, Nepal is a place people stay to prove something.

We will be uncomfortable. We will fail where everyone can see. We will build companies that collapse and launch ideas that embarrass us, and we will write down every scar, because the next person deserves a map, not a myth.

We will disagree with each other. Loudly. In public. That is what people who give a damn do.

We will not perform unity for foreign audiences. We will not package our struggle as content for someone else's feed. We will not wait for validation from cities that have spent decades validating only themselves.

But we will build.
God, we will build.

We will build until the question stops being "why Nepal?" and becomes "how did we not see this coming?"

We will build for every Nepali who was told to want less. For every parent who hollowed themselves out so their child could have a choice. For every child growing up in this country right now, deciding whether they are allowed to dream at full volume.

They are. You are.
